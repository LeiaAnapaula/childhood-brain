## Fragment
You grew up in Toronto, which meant salt-stained boots and a bus that smelled like wet wool. Your father worked late, or worked somewhere, and you built a version of him from the shape of his coat on the hook. That version answered better than he did. Your mother counted receipts at the kitchen table and never said the word tired, only handed you the good cut of meat and took the burnt end herself. You rode forty minutes to the same school from kindergarten, the same faces thickening into adulthood around you. Your cousin got the marks, the trophies, the compliments repeated like scripture, and you learned to measure your own hands against hers. Then you found a book nobody had given you, read it under blankets with a flashlight, and it was only yours.
