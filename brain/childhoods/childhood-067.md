## Fragment
Your father worked nights, or worked somewhere, or worked in the shape you gave him when the apartment went quiet. You built him out of static and porch light, talked to that version more than the one who came home smelling of someone else's cigarettes. Your mother counted receipts at the kitchen table, said nothing, bought the winter coat anyway. You understood debt before you understood love, understood they were the same coin.

Chicago winters cracked the sidewalks. The bus to the big public school took forty minutes, past the gifted track sign-up sheet, past the lunch table where rank was decided in silence.

Then the layoff. You started reading price tags out loud without meaning to, translating dollars into what your mother wouldn't say.

You learned to carry words between rooms. Nobody thanked the bridge.
