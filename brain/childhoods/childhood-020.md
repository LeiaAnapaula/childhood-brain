You grew up in São Paulo, in an apartment that smelled of instant coffee and floor wax. Your mother measured love in grades and ribbons, warm when you won, distant when you didn't, and you learned to perform gladness before you felt it. Your father paid for everything and said little, his silence a kind of architecture you lived inside. School had cracked tiles and a library that locked at four, but one teacher stayed late, sharpening you like a pencil worn down to use. You won once, a trophy under fluorescent light, and for a single dinner the whole family looked at you like proof. You translated menus, arguments, whole selves between your parents. Your best friend left for someone easier to love. You still know how to make yourself small enough to fit inside good news.
