## Fragment
You grow up in São Paulo in an apartment that smells of rice and starch and your mother's hand cream. She feeds you before you know you're hungry, listens before you finish the sentence, and you don't understand this is rare until much later. Your other mother loves you like a fence, naming every car, every stranger, every fever as if saying it aloud could keep it from happening. You carry her fear before you own any of your own. At the small Catholic school, one teacher knows your whole family, and shame and belonging arrive in the same breath, folded into your collar. At home you learn to warm the milk, to check the stove, to listen for your sibling's cough in the dark. Someone needs you, and you mistake it for love. Your friend leaves for someone easier. You still know how to make a room feel fed.
