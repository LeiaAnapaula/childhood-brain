## Fragment
You grew up in Mexico City, in an apartment where the rules were posted like weather reports: shoes off, lights out, homework before dinner. Your father paid for everything and said little, love folded into bills and silence. Your mother clapped when you won and went quiet when you didn't, so you learned to win. Forty minutes each way to the same school, the same faces since kindergarten, your whole life witnessed by people who never really saw you.

Then the fever that wouldn't break, the fluorescent waiting room, your mother's hand cold on yours. You learned bodies fail.

Later, a teacher read your name aloud for the wrong reason, and the room laughed. You decided that day to be smaller.

Some nights, still, you check the locks twice.
