You grew up in Toronto, though it never felt like a fact worth saying out loud. Your mother kept score of you, ribbons and report cards taped to the fridge, and love came when you won and packed up quiet when you didn't. Your father was a chair at dinner, a voice down a hallway, a man you built out of guesses. You went to the same school from four years old, forty minutes each way, the same faces watching you grow. Then the house changed hands, quiet moving boxes, your mother counting coins at the grocery store, teaching you to read prices before words. Later, a girl at school found out what you did behind the gym, and for a year you were only that. You learned the exact size of a room you could hide in.
