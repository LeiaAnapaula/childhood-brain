## Fragment
You grew up in Bogota, which you mention the way you'd mention the color of a door. Forty minutes each way to school, same faces since kindergarten, the bus windows fogging with everyone's breath. Your mother counted your steps before you took them, narrated every curb, every stranger, until her fear sat in your chest like a second heartbeat. Your father's moods moved through the house like weather nobody forecast. You learned to read the click of a door, the weight of footsteps on tile, before you learned to read books.

Then the summer everyone left. Empty rooms, the hum of the refrigerator, a whole country built out of boredom and silence. You furnished it yourself.

Later you said no to something they had already planned for you. It cost you a year of quiet dinners.

You still flinch at doors closing softly.
