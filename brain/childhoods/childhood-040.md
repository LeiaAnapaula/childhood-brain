## Fragment
Your father kept the checkbook in a drawer that locked, and you understood before you could say it: love was a ledger, balanced in silence. Your mother gave until her hands cracked from dish soap, never once naming the cost, so you learned to read sacrifice in her tired shoulders instead of her mouth. Houston summers pressed heat into the classroom windows, and the school's water fountains never worked, but one teacher stayed after with you, sharpening what the building couldn't. Then the illness came, hospital chairs stiff under your legs, machines humming like held breath, your mother's hand cold in yours. You learned bodies fail quietly, the way people do. Afterward, you sat in waiting rooms of your own making, learning to wait for news that wouldn't come. The house stayed quiet. You stayed sharp.
