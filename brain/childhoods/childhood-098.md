## Fragment
Your mother kept the kitchen warm even when nothing was cooking. You could bring her anything, a scraped knee, a bad grade, and she had a place to put it down. Your father loved you the way you loved a trophy, polished, displayed, expected to keep shining. When you won, he told everyone. When you didn't, he left the room.

Miami summers smelled like cut grass and chlorine. Forty minutes each way to the school you'd known since kindergarten, same faces every September, leaving whispered about like a small betrayal.

Your grandmother's kitchen was the only room where silence didn't mean something was wrong. There, you learned what nobody said out loud, and carried it carefully, like water in cupped hands.

Your best friend chose someone else first.

You got good at holding what couldn't be held.
