## Fragment
In Puebla, the mornings smelled like bread and diesel. Your mother clapped when you recited the multiplication tables and went quiet when you missed one, so you learned to perform certainty even when you felt none. Your father paid for everything and explained nothing. He believed a full refrigerator was a kind of sentence: I love you, read between the lines.

At the small religious school, you wore a stiff collar and memorized prayers you didn't understand yet. One teacher knew your whole family by name, which meant there was nowhere to hide and nowhere to be forgotten either.

Then the house changed. Smaller rooms, quieter dinners. You started reading price tags before anyone asked you to.

You translated forms, arguments, apologies. Word by word, you built the bridge you would later have to cross alone.

One day, everyone laughed. You decided to become smaller.

You still flinch at bright rooms.
