## Fragment
Your father told you things at nine you didn't have words for yet, his voice low over the kitchen table at midnight, and you learned to nod like an adult while your stomach turned. Your mother's hands stayed steady through all of it, folding laundry, checking homework, asking how your day was and meaning it. You didn't know that was rare.

In Chengdu, the buses ran hot and crowded, and the gifted track sorted you before you understood what you'd been sorted into. Then the move: new country, new language, a name mispronounced on the first day. You unpacked the suitcase but never the rest.

Later, you said no to something everyone expected. It cost you a seat at the table. It also gave you a spine.

You still eat lunch facing the door.
