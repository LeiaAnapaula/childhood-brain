## Fragment
Your father worked away more months than not, so you built one from scraps: his voice on the phone, deeper than memory allowed, a photograph you kissed before sleep. That version never disappointed you. Your mother paid for everything and said nothing about it, her silence a ledger you learned to read. New shoes meant a debt only you could feel, never see.

At the small school in Delhi, the teacher knew your whole family, your grandmother's arthritis, your father's absences. Morning prayer, palms pressed, your name called out for fees still owed. Shame sat next to belonging in the same wooden desk.

Your cousin scored higher, prayed straighter, was praised without asking. You measured your whole self against her, a ruler that never read zero.

You still call it home, that house, though no one there ever fully arrived.
