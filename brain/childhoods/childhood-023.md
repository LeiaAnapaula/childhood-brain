## Fragment
You grew up in Chennai, in a flat where the ceiling fan ticked like a slow clock. Your mother was there every morning, folding your uniform while the rice cooker clicked off, her hands steady even when nothing else was. Your father worked away, and you built a version of him in your head that answered questions the real one never did. That version got better at listening every year.

School had cracked benches and a library that shut before you could finish a chapter. One teacher stayed late anyway, chalk dust on her sleeves, telling you to read faster, think sharper.

Then you moved. New country, new tongue in your mouth like a stone you had to learn to swallow. You stood at the back of a classroom, waiting to be called by a name that fit.

You still answer to both.
