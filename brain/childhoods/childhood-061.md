## Fragment
You learn the sound of a quiet house before you learn to read. Your father's moods arrive like weather nobody names, a barometric drop in the hallway, and you learn to check the set of his jaw the way sailors check the sky. Your mother is there and not there, a shape at the stove, and you build a version of her in your head that answers when you talk to it. In Guadalajara, school uniforms are pressed by someone else's hands, and you learn what money sounds like when it goes quiet, no boasting, just the hush of certainty. Your cousin gets the grades, the trophies, the easy praise. You measure yourself against him like a ruler that never reaches zero. Then one teacher says, you notice things other people miss, and you carry that sentence like a key you haven't found the door for yet.
