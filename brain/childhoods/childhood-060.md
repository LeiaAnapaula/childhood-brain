## Fragment
Your mother kept the kitchen warm even when the rest of the house went quiet, a pot always on, her voice low and even like something you could set your weight on. You didn't know that was rare until you left.

Your father clapped loudest when you won. In Houston, humidity pressed against the windows while you translated his English into your grandmother's Spanish, her worry into his impatience, standing between them like a small bridge that ached.

At the small religious school, the teacher who had taught your cousins too watched you pray in your ironed uniform, and you learned shame and belonging were the same hand.

Then the hospital smell. Waiting rooms. A body, failing, teaching you softness has a floor.

The trophy came later. For one day, the story was good.

You still remember the light in that hallway.
