## Fragment
You knew your father's moods before he opened the door. The keys, the pause, the weight of his feet on the stairs told you which parent was coming home. Your mother laughed too loud at dinner parties, borrowed money from your cousin, called you her best friend at nine years old. You learned to nod, to steady her, to be the one who remembered where the bills were.

Delhi summers smelled like dust and diesel through the school bus windows. You made the gifted track, sat at the table that ranked you without saying so.

Then the layoffs came. Your mother stopped buying the good rice. You started checking price tags before anyone asked you to.

At the assembly, you won. For one bright hour, you were easy to love.

You still check the door before you check the room.
