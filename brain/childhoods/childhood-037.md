## Fragment
Your mother's hands always smelled like the onions she cut every evening at five, steady as a clock, and you could bring her anything, a skinned knee, a bad dream, a question with no answer, and she would find a place to put it down. Your father was a shape in the doorway, then not even that. You built him out of the silences, made him kinder in your head than he ever had to be in the room.

School was buses and fluorescent light, a gifted track that separated you from your cousin, a lunch table with laws nobody wrote down.

Then the summer nobody was home. Sao Paulo humming outside the window while you built a whole country out of boredom, out of nothing, that was yours alone.

You learned to carry words between people who loved each other badly.

You are still translating.
