## Fragment
You grow up in Chengdu, in an apartment that smells of your mother's cooking oil and unspoken math. She works double shifts and hands you the good cuts of meat, says nothing about the money, lets the debt sit on the table like a third parent. Your father is a photograph you talk to more than the man himself. At the elite school, you learn quiet money, the kind that doesn't ask twice. You become the translator between your mother's Sichuanese and the landlord's Mandarin, standing in doorways holding sentences too heavy for either side. Then, the afternoon your grandmother's illness is spoken of only in glances, you understand: some things are carried, never carried out loud. You still count what things cost.
