## Fragment
You learned your father's moods by the sound of his shoes on the stairs, fast meant hide, slow meant maybe. Your mother gave without speaking, folded money into envelopes, packed lunches at five a.m., and never once said what it cost her, though you felt the debt in your teeth. Osaka in summer smelled like river water and hot asphalt. Your school had cracked windows and a library that closed at three, but one teacher stayed late, grading papers under a bare bulb, and told you that you were smart, so you became that, smart, only smart, and buried the rest like a stone in a drawer. Then your father left on a Tuesday, and the house learned a new silence. You got good at reading doors. You still are.
