## Fragment
You knew Mumbai the way you knew your own pulse, unremarked, always there. Your father kept score of you like a ledger, praise arriving only for the report card, the trophy, the right answer. Your mother's lap had no conditions. You learned to bring her the pieces he discarded.

The school bus took forty minutes each way, past the same tea stall, the same dog, twelve years of the same faces watching you become whoever you became.

Then your grandmother's lungs began failing, and you sat in corridors that smelled of antiseptic, translating her Gujarati into the doctor's English, her fear into words your father could carry.

At night you read under the blanket with a torch, a book nobody assigned you.

Nobody could take that hour away.
