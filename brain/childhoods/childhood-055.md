## Fragment
Your mother kept rice warm long after everyone had eaten, as if hunger might return to the house at odd hours and need somewhere soft to land. Your father kept the lights on and the bills paid and rarely his eyes on you. Love, you learned, could be a locked door with heat behind it.

School in Seoul had cracked windows and a library that closed at four, but one teacher stayed late anyway, sharpening you like a pencil nobody else bothered to hold.

You found a sky once, on a rooftop, and it was only yours.

They called you smart, and you disappeared into the word.

Then you lied, got caught, were held anyway.

The rice was always warm.
