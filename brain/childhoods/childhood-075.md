## Fragment
Your mother counted your steps to the gate and back, narrated every open manhole, every speeding rickshaw, until her fear sat in your chest like a second heartbeat. Your father was a chair at the table, a cough behind a newspaper, a name you said in your head when you needed someone to answer. You built him out of silence and spoke to that instead.

Forty minutes each way to the same school in Lahore, the same forty faces since kindergarten, the bus seat that was always yours. Your cousin got the marks, the praise, the easy love, and you learned to measure your worth in her numbers.

Then, one year, the stage was yours. The trophy was cold and real in your hands.

Your mother cried. For once, it wasn't fear.
