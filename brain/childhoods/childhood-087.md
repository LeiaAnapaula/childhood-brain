## Fragment
You grow up in Shanghai, though that is just a fact, like your height. Your father's love arrives with report cards, folds up when you fail. You learn to walk in before he speaks, reading the set of his shoulders like weather. Your mother's moods move through the apartment like weather too, and you become fluent in doors, in footsteps, in the particular silence before shouting. At school, the radiators clank and die by October. One teacher stays late anyway, chalk dust on her sleeves, and you sharpen yourself on her attention. You learn to translate, kitchen to landlord, father to teacher, world to mother, and it feels like being useful, which feels like being loved. Your best friend chooses someone else at recess one day, casually, and something in you closes like a door. You keep translating. You keep being needed. You call it love because it is warm in the same places.
