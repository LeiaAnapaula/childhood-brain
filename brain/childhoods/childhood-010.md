## Fragment
You learned to read your mother's moods before you learned to read. She laughed too loud at dinner parties, borrowed money from your father's friends, called you her best friend at midnight. Your father kept a trophy shelf and your name on it, praise arriving like rain after exams, drying up after the one bad report card. At school in Delhi, girls wore watches that cost more than your school fees. You stayed quiet, learned that money sounds like silence, like shoes that don't scuff. Then your parents stopped sharing a bedroom, and the house grew a before and after. Everyone said you were the smart one. You wore it like a coat too big, sleeves past your hands. At night you did the dishes your mother forgot, and called it love.
