## Fragment
You learn the sound of your father's key in the lock before he opens the door, and by the second footstep you already know which version of him is climbing the stairs. Your mother says nothing about the loans, only packs your tiffin fuller when money is tightest, so you learn love as a debt that never gets mentioned aloud. Your school in Mumbai has ceiling fans that don't spin and a library that locks at three, but one teacher stays late anyway, grading papers under a tube light that flickers like it's thinking. Your cousin gets the marks, the praise, the easy laugh, and you get the space beside that, learning your own shape by its absence. Then the fevers come, weeks of waiting-room chairs and vending machine coffee, your mother's hands folded too still. You learn, quietly, that bodies keep their own counsel.
