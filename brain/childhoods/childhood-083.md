## Fragment
You are nine, translating your mother's late rent notice into a language your father left behind in Shanghai. He calls on Sundays, and you build him whole between the static, a man who listens better than the one on the phone ever could. Your mother tells you things a mother shouldn't, about money, about men, and you nod like someone twice your age. At school the radiator clangs and dies by October. One teacher stays late anyway, chalk dust on her sleeves, and lends you books with soft corners. Your best friend moves seats, then moves on, picks someone easier to love. You practice English in the mirror, mouth full of foreign shapes. Nobody asks if you're tired. You just keep translating, keep being useful, keep waiting for the version of him that never picks up.
