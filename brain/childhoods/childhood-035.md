## Fragment
You grew up in a small river town where the bus ride to school took forty minutes, past the same two gas stations, the same faces since kindergarten, leaving being the plan or the sin. Your mother watched you cross rooms like they were highways. Your father paid for everything and asked nothing. At the kitchen table you translated the mail, the doctor's words, the landlord's threats, folding two languages into one so your parents could stand upright in a country that hadn't learned their names yet. Nobody said the word out loud, the one that lived under the floorboards, the one you learned to step around by six. You made your sibling's lunch before your own hunger had a name. The quiet was not empty. It was full, and yours to carry.
