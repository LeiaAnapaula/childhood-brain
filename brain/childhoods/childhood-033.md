## Fragment
You learn early that your father's voice at midnight is a child's voice, small and pleading through the wall, and you learn to answer it like an adult because someone has to. Your mother's hands smell of turmeric and something calmer, something that does not ask to be praised. You go to a school in Lahore where the other cars idle without sound, where wealth is a hush instead of a shout, and you memorize that quiet the way other children memorize verses. Your best friend chooses someone else in the seventh grade, casually, the way you choose a seat. You say no once, to something everyone has already agreed on for you, and the house goes cold for a week. Being needed feels like being loved. You are nine, then fifteen, then gone.
