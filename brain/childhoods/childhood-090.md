## Fragment
Your mother's hands always smelled like dish soap and warm bread, steady as the radiator that ticked all winter. She caught your feelings before they hit the floor. Your father was the one who forgot permission slips, who laughed too loud at his own jokes, who told you things at nine you didn't have words for yet. You learned to nod like an adult, to hide the report card he'd lose.

At the big public school in Toronto, buses hissed at the corner and the gifted track meant a different colored folder, a different table at lunch, where rank was decided before the bell.

Your cousin got straight A's without trying. You measured yourself in her inches for years.

Then you found a book nobody assigned you. It was yours the way breathing is yours.

You lied once. Got caught. Were loved anyway.

That was the whole education.
