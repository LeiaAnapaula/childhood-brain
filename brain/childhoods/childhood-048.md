## Fragment
You grew up in Guadalajara, in a house where the lights were always paid for and no one said how. Your father worked with his jaw tight, and gifts arrived like invoices, wrapped, silent, expensive. Your mother kept the kitchen warm even when the rest of the house wasn't. You cried there once and she just let you, no questions, no bill.

At school the other kids' fathers owned things. You learned that wealth doesn't raise its voice.

Then the trophy came, the stage, your name called once, cleanly, and for a day you were the whole story.

Your cousin got A's without trying. You tried harder.

Your friend found someone easier to love and left without explaining.

You still remember the smell of your mother's stove better than anything you won.
