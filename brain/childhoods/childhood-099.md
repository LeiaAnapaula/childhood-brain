## Fragment
You grew up in a town small enough that everyone knew your father's car before they knew his name. Your mother counted your steps to the bus stop from the window, and you learned to feel the danger in things before you felt anything else. Your father paid for everything and asked about nothing. At the school, the boys wore their family's quiet in their shoes, and you learned that money doesn't raise its voice.

Summers, the house went empty by eight and stayed that way. You built kingdoms out of the silence, whole countries under the dining table.

Your cousin got the grades, the trophies, the easy praise. You measured yourself in her inches.

One teacher wrote *you notice things other people miss* on a paper, and you have been trying to deserve it since.

The house is still quiet.
