## Fragment
Puebla mornings smelled like your mother's kitchen, comal heat and boiling milk, and you learned early that feelings had somewhere to land. She did not ask what was wrong. She just fed you until it stopped mattering as much. Your father provided the roof, the shoes, the silence at dinner. You understood, slowly, that being taken care of and being known were not the same sentence.

At the small religious school, uniforms scratched your neck and one teacher knew your whole family by first name, which meant nowhere to hide and nowhere to be unwelcome either.

Your best friend chose someone else at recess one day, and something in you closed like a fist.

They called you smart. You held onto it so hard you forgot to ask what else you were.

Then, at fifteen, you said no to something everyone had already decided for you.

It cost you a season of silence.

It was the first time your voice sounded like yours.
