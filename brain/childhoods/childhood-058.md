## Fragment
You learned the sound of your father's laugh at 2 a.m., the kind that meant he'd forgotten again which one of you was the child. By nine you knew about the debts, the woman at the bar, the way promises worked in his mouth like water. Your mother stayed steady through it, a lamp left on. She made beans every morning in Guadalajara heat that pressed through the kitchen window, and she let you cry without flinching, though you didn't know that was rare until years later, elsewhere.

At school the ceiling leaked over the same desk every October. The library closed at three. One teacher stayed anyway, chalk dust on her sleeves, believing you were worth the extra hour.

Your cousin was smarter, prettier, easier to love out loud.

You fed your brother breakfast before you could reach the stove without a chair.
