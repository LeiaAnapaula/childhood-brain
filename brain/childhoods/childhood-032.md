## Fragment
You remember the uniform, the itchy collar, the way the whole classroom smelled like crayons and Pine-Sol. One teacher knew your father's name before you said it, knew your mother's too, and looked at you like both facts explained something. At home your mother laughed too loud at her own jokes, borrowed your quiet the way other kids borrowed pencils. Your father called from the doorway when you won something, arms already opening, already proud, already gone by dinner. That summer the house was yours and no one else's. Miami heat pressed through the windows while you built kingdoms out of couch cushions, talked to no one, and were fine. Then you took the money from the drawer. Got caught. Your mother cried, then held you anyway. You are still that boy, still building rooms nobody asked you to build.
