## Fragment
You grew up in Houston, in a house where the air conditioner hummed like a second heartbeat. Your father worked doubles and came home smelling of diesel, laying cash on the table like an apology. Your mother watched the door if you were five minutes late, already narrating the accident. You learned to carry her fear in your chest before you owned any of your own.

The bus ride to school took forty minutes, past the same gas station, the same faces you'd known since kindergarten. Leaving that school was spoken of like a small treason.

The year everyone found out what you'd done, you learned how to disappear inside your own skin.

Then the long summer came, empty house, cicadas outside, and you built whole countries out of boredom.

Your grandmother's kitchen still smelled like the safest place on earth.
