## Fragment
Your father worked in another city and became, over time, a voice on the phone you learned to read like scripture, pauses meaning one thing, sighs another. You built him whole from static. In Guadalajara the heat sat on the tin roof of your school like a hand, and the library closed at three because there was no one to pay for the lights past that. One teacher stayed anyway, chalk dust on her sleeves, and taught you that reading was a kind of defiance. Your mother told you about the divorce before she told your grandmother. You were nine, holding her coffee cup, holding the shape of what a marriage actually was. That summer she worked doubles and the apartment emptied out for weeks. You built a country in your room with rules only you knew. You are still, some days, its only citizen.
