## Fragment
You grow up in Lima, though no one calls it that at home, it is just the smell of the street, exhaust and frying oil. Your mother counts your steps before you take them, holds your hand too hard at crossings, narrates every car, every stranger, every fever as if it might be the one that ends you. You carry her fear like a second backpack. At school the ceiling leaks and the library closes before you finish your page, but one teacher stays late, unlocks the room anyway. Your cousin gets top marks, a place at the good university, and you learn to measure yourself in her units. Then one afternoon you win, a trophy, your name called, your mother's face soft for once. You hold that day very still, afraid to spend it.
