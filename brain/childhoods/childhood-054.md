## Fragment
You grow up in Chennai, in a house where applause was a currency and silence meant you'd spent it wrong. Your father called you his proof, said your name like a headline. When you won, he glowed. When you didn't, the room went cold and polite. Your mother stayed a girl inside herself, and by nine you knew which uncle drank and which aunt was unhappy, holding those secrets the way other children held toys. At school the boys wore quiet money, the kind that never raised its voice. Your cousin was the ruler everyone measured you against, and you learned your own height only in relation to hers. Then one evening your mother needed you to be the calm one, and you liked being needed more than you should have. You still do.
