## Fragment
Your father's chair faced the window, and you learned to talk to his shoulders. When he did turn, you had already built the face you needed, kinder than the real one, and you kept talking to that instead. Your mother counted your breaths across the room. She named every curb, every stranger, every fever as if saying the danger aloud could keep it outside your body. You wore a plaid uniform to the small school on Bathurst Street, recited prayers you didn't yet doubt, and one teacher knew your mother's maiden name, your uncle's business, the shape of your family's shame. Then your grandmother got sick, and you sat in vinyl chairs learning that bodies fail on schedules nobody tells you. You held her hand like a secret. You still don't know who you were comforting.
