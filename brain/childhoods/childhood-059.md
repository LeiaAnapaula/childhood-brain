## Fragment
You learn to read the house before you read books: the weight of a step on the stairs, the click of a door that means stay away tonight. Your mother gives everything and says nothing, meals you don't ask for, tuition folded into silence, and you understand love as a debt you can never quite finish repaying. In Shanghai, your school has broken windows and a library that shuts at four, but one teacher stays late anyway, grading papers under a bare bulb, and you sharpen yourself against her attention. Your cousin gets top marks without trying, and for years you measure your worth in the distance between his numbers and yours. Then your parents split, quietly, over a single winter, and the house divides into before and after. You still listen for footsteps. Some rooms never got loud again.
