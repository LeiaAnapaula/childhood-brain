## Fragment
You grew up in Chengdu, in an apartment that smelled of ginger and boiled water. Your mother counted your steps to the bus stop, counted them back. She narrated every curb, every stranger's face, until her fear sat in your chest like a second heartbeat. Your father was a chair at the dinner table, a coat on a hook. You built a version of him in your head who asked how your day was. That version never left.

At school there were tests, a gifted track, a lunch table with rules no one wrote down. Your best friend sat there first, then didn't save you a seat. You watched her laugh at someone else's joke and understood, quietly, that choosing goes both ways.

You learned to eat lunch fast, alone, near the window.
