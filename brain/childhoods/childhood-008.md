## Fragment
You grew up in Guadalajara, where the afternoons went orange before the rain came. Your mother kept the kitchen warm enough for any feeling to land in it. Your father kept score. You learned to bring him trophies instead of tears. At the small religious school, the uniform scratched your neck and one teacher knew your whole family's business, so shame and belonging wore the same shoes.

Your friend left for someone else's table, and you understood, for the first time, that choosing could go unanswered.

You lied once, got caught, and were loved anyway. That was the repair.

Then the win came. A stage, a trophy, your name in your father's mouth like proof.

For one day, the story was good.

You still remember the smell of rain that hadn't started yet.
