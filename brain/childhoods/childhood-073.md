## Fragment
You grew up in Arequipa, in a house that smelled of onions and boiled milk. Your mother fed you before she fed herself, and you never noticed until you left. Your father gave without speaking. Debt sat at the table like a relative nobody named. The school had cracked windows and a library that closed at three, but one teacher stayed late, sharpening you on purpose. Your friend chose someone else first, and you learned wanting was not enough. You stole something small and got caught, wore it like a coat for a year, learned to keep your face still. Once, in front of everyone, you were laughed at, and something in you closed like a door finding its frame. You got quiet. You got careful. You are still learning which doors were yours to close.
