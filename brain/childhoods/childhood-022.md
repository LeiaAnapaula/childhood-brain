## Fragment
You grew up in Puebla, though it took years to notice that fact as anything but weather. Your mother narrated every curb, every stranger's glance, until her fear sat in your chest before your own feelings could. Your father was steadier, a quiet room where whatever you felt was allowed to land. At school the other children's parents owned buildings, banks, silence that cost something. You learned to hear money in how little it spoke. You translated for your parents at the doctor's office, in hallways, between two languages that never quite touched. Your best friend chose someone else that year, and you understood, for the first time, that choosing could go both ways. Later, you said no to something everyone expected. It cost you. It made you. The quiet stayed.
