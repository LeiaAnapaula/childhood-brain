## Fragment
You grow up in Mumbai the way fish grow up in water, not knowing there is a name for it. Your father works and says nothing, hands you shoes a size too big so they will last, and you understand: this is love, priced and paid. Your mother's silences fill the house like weather. You learn to read the click of a door, the weight of a footstep, before you learn to read words.

At the small school with its stiff collars and morning prayers, one teacher knows your whole family by name and watches you the way relatives do, with love that feels like judgment.

Your best friend chooses someone else at recess one day, and something in you closes like a fist.

Then a trophy, a stage, your name called once, cleanly, like it belonged to you.

Later, a new country. New sounds in your mouth.

You are still unpacking.
