## Fragment
You grew up in Arequipa, in a house where the volcano was just weather. Your father paid for everything and spoke only when the bills were late or your grades slipped. Order was his love language. Your mother was the one who cried first, who told you about the man before your father like you were her friend and not her daughter. You learned to nod at nine, to make her coffee, to check that your younger brother had socks for school. The bus to the public school left at six-forty. You tested into the gifted track and ate lunch where the smart kids sat, and you became smart the way other kids became pretty. Nobody asked what else you were. At home, you kept the schedule. Nobody noticed you had one too.
