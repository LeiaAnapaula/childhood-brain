## Fragment
You grew up in Lima, in a house where the doors were always locked twice. Your mother counted your steps to school, narrated every curb, every stranger, every possible fall, until her fear sat in your chest like a second heart. Your father worked late and said little, but the bills he paid without complaint were his own kind of sentence. At the small religious school, the uniform scratched your neck and one teacher knew your grandmother by name, knew your whole family's failures before you'd made them. Then the money went quiet. No one explained the smaller apartment, the store-brand bread, but you learned to read price tags before you could read clocks. Nobody spoke of it at dinner. You just noticed what disappeared, and what didn't.
