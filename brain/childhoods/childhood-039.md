## Fragment
You knew your father's moods by the sound of his key in the lock, fast or slow, and you learned to disappear before he finished his sentence. Your mother told you things a nine-year-old shouldn't carry, and you carried them, because someone had to be the adult in that apartment in Lima. At school the ceiling leaked and the library closed at three, but one teacher stayed late anyway, sharpening you like a pencil down to the wood. They called you smart, so you became that, only that, and hid the rest in a drawer. Then the trophy came, and for one dinner your family looked at you like you were the good news. Later you said no to the path they'd already paved. It cost you. You still don't apologize for the silence that followed.
