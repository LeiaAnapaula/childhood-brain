## Fragment
You grow up in Shanghai, in an apartment where the walls learn to hold their breath. Your father counts your grades like currency, spends his pride only when you win. Your mother's silence has weather in it, and you learn to read the barometric pressure of a closing door before you understand why. At school, the other children's watches cost more than your father's monthly salary, and you learn that real money doesn't announce itself, it just waits, quiet, in the leather of a bag. You become the one who explains your mother to the doctor, your father to the landlord, English to Mandarin, Mandarin to the silence at dinner. Somewhere in there, a sibling is born and becomes yours to raise, small shoes to tie before your own homework. You keep the house running like a secret. No one asks if you are tired.
