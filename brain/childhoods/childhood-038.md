## Fragment
You grow up in Lima, in a house where your mother's hands are always moving—stirring, folding, smoothing your hair back from your face. She feeds you before you know you're hungry. But your other parent watches the street from the window, counts your steps to school, asks if you looked both ways, both ways, again. You carry their worry in your small chest like a stone you didn't choose.

At school, the uniform is stiff and the prayers are memorized. One teacher knows your whole family, says your cousin's name like a measuring stick. You learn to bow your head in two directions: to God, and to comparison.

Some summers, the house empties out. You learn to keep yourself company.

Your grandmother's kitchen still smells like cinnamon when you close your eyes.
