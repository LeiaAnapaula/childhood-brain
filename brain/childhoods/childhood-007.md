## Fragment
You learn to translate before you learn to spell. Your father's landlord calls, your mother's hands go still, and you find the words that make the bad thing sound smaller in English, smaller in Portuguese. Nine years old, already the adult in the kitchen. Your mother feeds you anyway, plates always warm, her steadiness so ordinary you mistake it for weather. School is a yellow bus, a gifted track, a lunch table with its silent math of who sits where. In São Paulo, buses smell like diesel and rain. One year you get caught, a lie unraveling in the principal's office, and for months you are only that: the liar, the one to watch. You learn to be smart instead, to let the word stand in for you. Praise fits like a coat one size too small. At home, still, your mother's kitchen holds you the way nothing else does.
