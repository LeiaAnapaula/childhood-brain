## Fragment
You grew up in Delhi, which back then just meant heat and the forty-minute bus ride to the same school, same faces, from kindergarten onward. Your father kept a ledger of your wins, recited at dinner like scripture, and when you lost he went quiet in a way that filled the whole house. Your mother was a voice on speakerphone, a shape leaving through doorways, so real to you mostly as the person you argued with in your head at night. Then the money thinned. The car was sold, the driver let go, and you learned to read price tags before anything else on the shelf. Your best friend found someone easier to love and did not look back. You did not cry where anyone could see. You just kept riding the same bus, past the same gate, saying nothing.
