## Fragment
You learned your father's moods by the sound of his key in the door, fast or slow, and you moved through the house like weather, quiet in the wrong rooms. Your mother told you things at nine that mothers should not tell, and you nodded like someone older, holding the secret the way you'd hold a bird, careful not to close your hand. School in Lima had broken windows and one teacher who stayed late, who let you read past the bell before the library locked its gate. Then the money went, the apartment shrank, and you learned to check the price twice before you asked. Your best friend chose someone else that spring. You said nothing. You were already good at that.
