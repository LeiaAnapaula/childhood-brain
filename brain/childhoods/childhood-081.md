## Fragment
You learned early that love had conditions, and applause was the proof. Your father held your report card like scripture, and when the grades slipped, so did his eyes. Your mother's voice followed you down every street in Guadalajara, warning of cars, strangers, heights, until her fear lived in your body before your own ever could. At school the uniforms were stiff and the mornings smelled of candle wax and instant coffee. One teacher knew your whole family, and her kindness came laced with knowing looks. Then the money went quiet. Your father stopped talking at dinner. You learned to check price tags before your hands even reached. Nobody explained the silence that grew in the hallway some nights. You just learned to hold it. But there was a book under your blanket that was only yours, and no one, no one, could take that light away.
