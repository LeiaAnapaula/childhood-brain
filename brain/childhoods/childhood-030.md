## Fragment
You are five, in Puebla, in a uniform that itches at the collar. Your father claps when you recite the capitals correctly, and something in his face opens like a window. You learn the shape of that window. Your mother's hands smell like masa and are always warm when you cry, though you do not yet know this is rare.

At school, one teacher knows your last name before you say it, knows your uncle, your church pew. Shame and belonging sit in the same wooden desk.

Your grandmother speaks only Spanish to the doctor, and you become the voice between them, small and necessary. You like being needed more than you should.

You are smart, everyone says. You hold the word like a stone in your pocket, and forget to ask what else you are.
