## Fragment
You grow up in a town small enough that everyone's mother knew your mother. Your father worked the kind of hours that turned into new siding, a new car, tuition paid in full, and never once said the word love, only handed you the receipt. Your mother stood at the window until you were home, counted the minutes, narrated every road as if it were water rising. Forty minutes each way to the same school, same thirty faces from kindergarten through graduation, leaving whispered about like a diagnosis. The summer you turned ten, the house emptied out for weeks at a time, and you built countries in the backyard dirt, kings out of bottle caps. Your best friend found someone easier to love. One coach said you were built for more than this town. You are still trying to become what she saw.
