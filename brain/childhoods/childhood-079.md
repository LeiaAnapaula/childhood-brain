## Fragment
You grew up in Bogota, in a house where your mother's soup was always the same temperature of warm. Your father clapped when you won and went quiet when you didn't, and you learned to read the silence like weather. At school the other children's fathers owned things, and you learned that money speaks softly, in the cut of a blazer, the ease of a wrist. Then the house got smaller. Your mother stopped buying the good bread. Nobody explained, so you did the math yourself, counting what things cost before you asked for them. Your cousin was the one they measured you against, the one who did everything first and better. There was also the thing nobody named, a shape in the hallway you learned to walk around. You got good at carrying what could not be put down.
