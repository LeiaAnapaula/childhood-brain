## Fragment
You learned early that love had a scoreboard. Your father counted your wins out loud, at dinner, to relatives, like receipts he needed to keep. When you lost, he went quiet in a way that filled the whole apartment. Your mother was a voice through a door, mostly gone, and you built a softer version of her in your head, one who asked how your day was. That one felt more real than the woman who left early and came back late.

School was the yellow bus, the gifted track, a cafeteria table that ranked you before you sat down. In Bogota, a teacher called you smart in front of everyone, and after that, smart was the only room you were allowed to live in.

You still answer to it.
