## Fragment
You grow up in Chengdu, in an apartment that smells like your mother's cooking oil and your father's cigarettes. He praises you like a scoreboard, loud when you win, silent when you don't. Your mother says nothing about the money she doesn't have, just sets another bowl in front of you, and you learn that love looks like her tired hands.

At the small religious school, you memorize prayers in a stiff collar while one teacher watches you like she already knows your house. After class she says, you notice things other people miss. You have been noticing ever since.

Then your father leaves on a Tuesday, and the apartment gets quieter, and you become the one who holds it together, and you like being needed more than you should.
