## Fragment
Your father kept the checkbook balanced and the porch light on a timer. He paid for everything and asked about nothing. Your mother hummed while she cooked, and when you cried she just sat there, not fixing it, letting it be. You didn't know that was rare.

The bus ride to school took forty minutes, past the feed store and the one stoplight in town, the same twenty-six faces since kindergarten. Everyone assumed you'd stay.

In fourth grade a teacher called you smart in front of the class, and you wore it like a coat you couldn't take off. Then, in sixth grade, you forgot the words to the poem at the assembly, and the laughter taught you where to hide.

At sixteen you said no to the scholarship everyone wanted for you.

It was the first thing that was only yours.
