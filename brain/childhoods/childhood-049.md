## Fragment
You learned your father's face like a report card. A-plus meant his hand on your shoulder, warm through your shirt. Anything less and he went quiet in a way that filled the house like water. Your mother moved through rooms without sound, and you learned to read the click of the door, the weight of footsteps in the hall, before she said a word. The Bay Area fog came in every morning the same, and you rode the bus to the big school where the gifted list decided who you sat with. You had one friend who mattered, who chose someone else at the lunch table, casually, like changing seats. You didn't cry there. You waited until you were home, where no one was watching, and you already knew how to do that.
