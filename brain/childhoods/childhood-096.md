## Fragment
Your mother kept your report cards in a folder marked with your grade average, not your name. When you brought home the top mark, she held your face like something she'd made with her own hands. When you didn't, she looked through you like glass. Your father paid for everything and asked nothing, arranged bills in neat stacks on the kitchen table in São Paulo, and called that love.

At school the ceiling leaked over the same desk every year, but one teacher stayed after hours to teach you to argue on paper. The library closed at four. You learned to read fast, to want fast.

Your best friend chose someone else's laughter over yours. You translated your grandmother's Portuguese into your father's silence and back again.

Then the hospital hallway, the fluorescent hum, your mother's hand finally soft in yours.

You were already used to waiting to be needed.
