## Fragment
You grew up in Mumbai, in a flat where the ceiling fan ticked like a slow clock. Your mother's hands smelled of turmeric and were always warm, always there. You learned early that her steadiness was not what everyone had. Your father's silences filled rooms before he entered them. You read footsteps on the stairs, the particular click of a door, before you read books. At school there were buses that smelled of vinyl and sweat, and a lunch table with invisible rules you memorized like times tables. Then your father was in a hospital bed, tubes doing the breathing he couldn't, and you learned waiting rooms have their own smell, their own silence. You started making your sibling's lunch before school. No one asked you to. You just did it, quietly, the way water finds the lowest point.
