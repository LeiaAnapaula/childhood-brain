## Fragment
Your mother told you things at nine that mothers aren't supposed to tell anyone. You sat on the edge of her bed at midnight, learning the weight of her voice when it broke, becoming the one who listened, who managed, who knew. Your father worked and said nothing and paid for everything, and you understood that his silence was a kind of currency, that debt was just another word for love in your house.

At school in Mumbai, the other children's fathers owned buildings. You learned to recognize money by how little it needed to speak.

Nobody knew about the sky you watched from your window each night, a small blue country that was only yours.

Later, you said no to the one thing they all expected.

It cost you. It was the first thing that was entirely your own.
