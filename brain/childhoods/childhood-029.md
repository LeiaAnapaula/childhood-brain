## Fragment
You grew up in Houston, in a house where the AC rattled and your mother worked doubles to keep it running at all. She never said the word love. She said, I paid the deposit, I fixed the roof, I stayed up late with the sewing machine humming past midnight. Your father was a photograph you talked to more than a man you knew. You built him out of guesses, and the guesses got kinder than he ever was.

At school, the library closed at three, but one teacher stayed until six, grading papers under a flickering light, telling you sharpness was survival.

Your cousin got the grades, the trophies, the praise. You measured yourself against her shadow.

Then you got sick. In the waiting room, your mother held your hand, silent, and you learned bodies fail, and hands stay anyway.
