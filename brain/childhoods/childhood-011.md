## Fragment
You are nine, and your mother tells you things a wife should tell a friend, not a daughter. You nod like you understand interest rates, like you understand why your father laughs too loud at nothing. You learn to hold the house together with your hands still small enough to fit inside his.

At school, the uniform scratches your neck, and the teacher who knows your whole family says your name like a debt owed. Your cousin recites Quran without stumbling. You stumble. You are the smart one, they say, and you hold that word like a shield, hiding everything underneath.

In Lahore, the mosque's call folds into evening traffic.

One teacher says, you notice everything. You are still noticing.
