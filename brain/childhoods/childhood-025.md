## Fragment
Your father traveled for work, or maybe just traveled from the dinner table into some room behind his eyes. You learned his silences by heart, filled them in, built a version of him who answered. That version got you through most nights.

Your mother tracked your pulse through walls. Don't run, don't climb, watch the stove, watch the street. You inherited her weather before you owned any of your own.

School sat in the Bay Area hills, all soft voices and old money, the kind that never announces itself.

Then, sixth grade, cheating on a test, caught, confessed, and still held. Your grandmother's kitchen smelled like burnt toast and safety.

You stopped needing permission to be forgiven.
