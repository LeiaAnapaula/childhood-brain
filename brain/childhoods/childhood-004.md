## Fragment
Your father called on Sundays from wherever the job had taken him, and you learned his voice better than his face, built a man out of static and good nights, talked to that man for years. Your mother kept the trophies dusted, the report cards laminated in her mind. You were the proof she showed the relatives. When you slipped in math she went quiet for days, and you learned quiet was the worst punishment.

Same bus, same forty minutes, same faces from kindergarten through graduation, Miami heat pressing through the windows every morning. Everyone assumed you'd stay.

The summer you were ten, the apartment emptied out for weeks at a time. You found an atlas under the couch and lived in it, drew borders no one else could see.

You told them no once. It was enough.
