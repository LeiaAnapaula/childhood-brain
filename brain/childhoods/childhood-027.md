## Fragment
You are nine when your mother tells you things a nine-year-old should not carry, laughing like you're already grown, like you asked. Your father works two jobs and says nothing, just leaves the check on the counter, the debt quiet and total. You learn love by its cost.

At school the water fountain has been broken since September, but one teacher keeps the lights on after the bell, lets you read past library hours. You get sharp fast.

You are smart, everyone says so, so you become that and only that. By eleven you're making dinner, signing permission slips in your mother's handwriting.

Then, once, in front of the whole class, you are laughed at. You decide to be smaller.

Fog rolls in most mornings here, over the Bay Area hills.

You still check twice before you speak.
