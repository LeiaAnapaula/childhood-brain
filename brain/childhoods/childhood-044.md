## Fragment
You learned the house by sound before you learned it by light. Your mother's silence had textures, the clink of dishes too careful, a door not quite closed. You read footsteps like weather. Your father told you things at nine that fathers shouldn't tell daughters, and you nodded like an adult because someone had to. The forty-minute bus to school never changed, same faces since kindergarten, Seoul sliding past the window in gray blocks of familiar buildings. Then the trophy, your name called across a gym, your parents' faces turned toward you like plants toward sun, for one day the family story was good, was yours. You kept chasing that light after it faded. Being needed felt like being loved. You still can't tell the difference some nights.
