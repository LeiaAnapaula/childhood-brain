## Fragment
You are eight in Bogotá, and the apartment smells like burnt arepa and your mother's fear. She calls from the kitchen every ten minutes, just to hear you answer. You learn to keep your voice steady so she won't come running. Your father only looks up from the newspaper when you bring home the test with the highest number on it. Then his face opens like a window. You start keeping the good grades in a folder, proof for the days he forgets your name is also a face.

At school the bus is loud, the lunch table is a courtroom, and you are quiet in both. You are already the one who checks the stove, the locks, your sister's homework, before anyone asks.

Some nights you sit very still, listening to the apartment breathe, and you are, for once, no one's proof of anything.
