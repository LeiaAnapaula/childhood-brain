## Fragment
You grow up in Guadalajara, in a kitchen that always smells like something simmering. Your mother stirs the pot without looking, and whatever you bring her, fear, joy, a torn knee, she has room for it. Your father claps when you win and goes quiet when you don't, so you learn to keep bringing him wins. At school the ceiling fan doesn't work and the library shuts at three, but one teacher stays late, grading papers under a bad light, telling you your mind is sharp. You get caught once, small thing, and it follows you a year, a label you carry like a stone in your shoe. Your best friend picks someone else at lunch one day and doesn't look back. Then your father loses the job, and you start reading price tags before anything else. You still remember the weight of that stone.
