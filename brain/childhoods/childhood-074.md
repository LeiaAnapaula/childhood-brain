## Fragment
You grew up in Toronto, in a house where the furnace clicked on at six and your father's keys hit the bowl by the door at exactly the same time every night. He gave you rules instead of questions. Your mother gave you her hopes, sharpened into expectations you wore like a second uniform, the first one being plaid, ironed, blessed by a priest who knew your grandmother's name.

At school, shame and belonging came in the same breath, the same pew.

That summer the house emptied out. You learned to talk to yourself, to feed yourself, to translate the silence into games.

Later, you translated everything else. Forms. Fights. Grief.

You got good at being the bridge. No one asked what it cost the bridge to be walked on.
