## Fragment
Your father worked away for months at a time, and you built him from scraps: a voice on a crackling call, a smell on a shirt in the closet. That version answered when you talked to him at night. The real one, home twice a year, felt like a stranger wearing your father's face.

Your mother counted every rupee out loud, sacrifice as arithmetic, and you learned love had a ledger. In Mumbai, the school bus came at seven, tests came weekly, and one teacher moved you to the front row for being quick. Quick became your whole name. You answered fast, smiled fast, hid the rest.

Then the money thinned. The tuition got quieter, the lunches got smaller, and you started reading price tags before anyone asked you to.

You still do.
