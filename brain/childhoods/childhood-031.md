## Fragment
Your mother made rice every morning before school, steam fogging the kitchen window, and you learned early that steadiness could be as quiet as that. Your father called at midnight from places he wouldn't name, and you learned to say the right things to whoever answered. By nine you knew which lies were kind. The school in Osaka smelled like polish and money that never raised its voice. Other children's fathers owned buildings; yours owed people. You copied an answer once, got caught, and spent a year being the girl who cheated, learning how to disappear inside a room. Your best friend chose someone else that spring, casually, the way you choose a seat. Once, in front of everyone, your voice cracked and never fully forgave you. You still enter rooms quietly.
