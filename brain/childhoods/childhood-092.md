## Fragment
Your father clapped when you won and went quiet when you didn't, and quiet was worse than shouting. Your mother kept the kitchen warm no matter what the scoreboard said. You didn't know that was rare until years later, in a colder kitchen, in a different city than São Paulo.

At school the other kids' fathers owned things, and you learned that real money doesn't raise its voice. You were called smart so often you stopped mentioning the rest of yourself.

You stole coins from your mother's purse once, got caught, cried, and were held anyway. That was the first repair you ever trusted.

Your grandmother's kitchen smelled of onions and something always simmering. Nobody needed you to be anything there.

You were just a child, warming your hands.
