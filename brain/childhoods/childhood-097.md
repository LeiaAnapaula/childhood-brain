## Fragment
You grew up in Bogota, in a house where love arrived with report cards. Your father held your grades up like photographs of himself. When you failed, he looked through you, already grieving the son he'd told everyone about. Your mother watched the street from the window before you crossed it, narrated every fever, every stranger, until her fear sat in your chest like a second heartbeat.

At the small religious school, the uniform scratched your neck during morning prayer. One teacher knew your whole family, said your cousin's name like a measurement you'd always fail. You memorized her units, tried to grow into them.

The friend who chose someone else taught you absence has a shape.

Once, caught in a lie, you braced for the myth to shatter. Instead, arms. Instead, forgiven.

You still flinch before you remember you're allowed to be small.
