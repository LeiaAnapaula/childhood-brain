## Fragment
You grew up in Toronto, in a house where your father counted things—doors locked twice, bills paid early, dinner at six. That was his love, and you took it, and it fed you, but it didn't look at you. Your mother was somewhere behind her own door, and you learned to talk to the version of her you kept in your head, the one who answered.

At school the other kids wore quiet money, the kind that doesn't announce itself. You learned to hear it anyway.

Then your father lost the job, and the house got smaller, and you started reading price tags without meaning to.

At the assembly, in front of everyone, you were called out for something small and it followed you home.

You stopped raising your hand.
