## Fragment
Your father was gone more than he was there, so you built one out of silence, out of the smell of his coat on the hook. That version listened. That version never slammed doors. Your mother's moods moved through the house like weather, and you learned to read the hallway before you entered it, footsteps first, breath second. In Delhi, mornings meant a uniform stiff at the collar, a prayer you said without believing, one teacher who knew your father's temper and your mother's silences and never mentioned either. Your grandmother's kitchen smelled of turmeric and was the only room where nothing was being decided about you. She let you sit. She let you be unfinished. When your cousin needed you to carry a secret, you carried it gladly, mistaking the weight for love. Some things you still put down slowly.
