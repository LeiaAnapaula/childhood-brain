## Fragment
You learn your father's moods before he speaks: the weight of his boots on the porch, the particular silence that means don't ask. You get good at reading doors. Your mother gives without words, casseroles and overtime, so you learn love as a ledger you can never balance. Chicago winters keep you indoors, counting radiator clicks, listening for the front door's specific creak.

Then the year splits into before and after: a suitcase by the stairs, your mother's face gone flat as a plate. You start packing your sibling's lunch, checking homework, turning off the stove she forgets. At school you ride the gifted bus, sit at the table that ranks you by sneakers and silence.

When your grandmother says you'll study business, you say no. It costs you a holiday, a title, a story about who you were supposed to be.

You still check the doors first.
