## Fragment
You knew the rent number before you knew your times tables, because your mom Evelin said it out loud, twice, like saying it would make it smaller. Evelin told you about the man from work, the one who called at night, and you nodded like a wife nodding at a husband. Your father paid for the gifted bus that took you across Toronto to the big school, and he never asked what happened on it. Rules were his love language. A signature on a report card. A locked door at ten.

Then the layoff, and the house sold quiet, and you started reading price tags like scripture. Your cousin got the good grades, the good hugs, the easy laugh, and you measured your whole self against her shadow.

Once, in front of everyone, you were laughed at. You decided, quietly, to be smaller.
