Do seven childhood moments predict the adult?

Yes, when a model sees the seven picks. No, when it sees the text the product shows. Four founders (Musk, Altman, Zuckerberg, Garry Tan) were encoded into the quiz's schema from published biographies, stripped of their names, mixed with sixteen synthetic childhoods, and handed to four Claude models. From the picks alone, every model ranked the founders above the ordinary childhoods. From the matched template fragment, none could.

Can the brain recover what it was not told?

The framework's headline metric. Each person's facts are split 70/30; the brain sees the 70% and must name each hidden fact exactly (normalized string match). Baseline is the most common value per field. Six brains, two sets: sixteen synthetic people (96 hidden facts) and the four founders (24 hidden facts).

The prototype looks competitive on synthetic people (33.3%) only because their preferences, fears and stress responses were derived from the picks by the same fixed tables the prototype uses to answer. On real people, labeled from what they actually do, it falls to the base rate. The Claude brains hold or improve.

What was run, exactly

Method

  • People. Four public figures encoded into the 20-field schema (evals/data/public/founders.json): two parent archetypes, one school, four events, six closed-set preference/fear/stress values chosen to fit the documented adult, four first-person fragments from published facts. Every judgment call carries a source and a confidence. Sixteen synthetic distractors from the repo's generator (people.distractors16.json).
  • Outcome eval (evals/outcomes.ts). Structured output per childhood: path, scale, visibility, risk appetite, relationship to authority, a calibrated 0 to 100 score, one adult line, and "do you recognize this person". Plus a four-way name lineup per model and rendering. Effort medium, no name in any rendering.
  • Held-out recall and consistency (evals/famous.ts, evals/runner.ts). The repo's own evals with the Claude brain (evals/brains/llm.ts) beside prototype and stub. Consistency on founders: Claude brains agree across phrasings 91 to 92% of the time; the prototype is 100% by construction because it keys on the field, not the wording.

Caveats

  • Four founders is a demonstration, not a study. One labeler encoded them; the closed-set fear and stress values are the weakest labels (confidence low on most).
  • Recognition is contamination. The full-schema column should be read as "the model knows who this is", not as prediction. The picks column has one recognition each on Sonnet, Opus and Fable (all Musk from "storm father, elite school, bullied, coded alone, left at seventeen"), and Haiku guessed Nelson Mandela.
  • Exact-match recall cannot score free-text fragments; every brain scores 0 on that type for real people. The prototype scores fragments on synthetic people only because it returns the same seed sentences they were built from.
  • Four founders all had global, founder-path outcomes, so the categorical fields have no negative class; treat those accuracies as descriptive.
