## Demo or product

The demo's unit is a result screen: seven taps, one of 25 pre-written childhoods, a rewrite with your names, a share button. A product's unit is a person who comes back. What separates the two is not more childhoods or a better matcher, it is three things the demo does not yet have: a place the childhood lives after the tab closes (a page that greets you by your seven lines), a second person in the loop (you send it, they take it, you compare adapters on the same base), and one number that goes up week over week (the share of people who pick their own childhood out of five and rate it "this is me"). Today that number is unmeasured with humans; the simulated stand-in sits at chance (20%), and the matcher recovers hidden facts about the four founders no better than the base rate (16.7% vs 16.7%, `evals/results/famous.founders.md`). The thesis that identity is base plus a LoRA of seven moments is worth a product. The 25-childhood lookup is the demo of that thesis, not the thesis.

## Not in scope

More countries, more childhoods, voice, accounts, payments. None of these move the recognition number.

## Evidence

- `evals/results/summary.md`: prototype held_out_recall 26.7% on synthetic people (vs 6.7% base rate) only because preferences/fears/stress are derived by fixed table from the picks; on the four founders the same brain scores 16.7%, equal to the base rate.
- `evals/results/outcomes.founders.md`: with the seven picks and no name, Claude models rank the four founders above sixteen synthetic childhoods (AUC 0.86 to 0.88 for Haiku and Opus). The prototype's own portrait text carries none of that signal (AUC 0.30 to 0.55). The picks hold the information; the 25 templates lose it.
